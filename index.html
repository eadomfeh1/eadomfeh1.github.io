01 / Research

Decisions people and machines make together

Understanding how humans and AI systems reach conclusions jointly — and designing that collaboration to be trustworthy when it matters most.

I am a PhD candidate in the Human in Computing and Cognition (THiCC) Lab at The Pennsylvania State University, advised by Dr. Christopher L. Dancy. My work sits within the NSF AI Institute for Societal Decision Making (AI-SDM), a federally funded institute dedicated to building human-centric AI for disaster management and public health.

The question that drives my research is deceptively simple: when an emergency manager has minutes to decide where to send rescue teams, and an AI system offers a recommendation — what actually happens? Not what the model computes, but how a person under pressure interprets, trusts, overrides, or defers to it. That interaction is where AI either saves lives or quietly fails. My systematic review of this space, published at IEEE ISTAS 2025, maps the patterns of human–AI collaboration across every phase of disaster management, and has been cited 25 times in its first year.

My dissertation work builds on that foundation across several threads: trust dynamics — how reliance on AI is calibrated over repeated interactions and what causes it to break down; human–AI collaboration — how to divide work between people and systems so each contributes what it does best; and the design of explanations that remain usable when time is short.

Before Penn State, my research spanned machine learning for medical diagnostics, ensemble methods for cybersecurity, and multi-criteria decision systems. The thread running through all of it is a concern with how computational methods serve human judgment in consequential settings — and with ensuring those systems work for everyone, including the communities disasters hit hardest.

01

Human-Centered AI

Designing AI systems around how people actually reason, decide, and build trust — rather than around idealized rational agents.

02

Trust Dynamics

How reliance on AI forms, erodes, and recalibrates across repeated interactions — and what keeps it appropriately calibrated.

03

Human–AI Collaboration

Dividing work between people and AI systems so each contributes what it does best, especially under time pressure.

04

Decision-Making Under Uncertainty

Disaster response settings where information is incomplete, time is short, and the stakes are lives.

05

Machine Learning

Model development and rigorous evaluation, from medical diagnostics to ensemble methods for threat detection.

06

Multi-Criteria Decision Making

Fuzzy AHP and optimization approaches for resource allocation and siting problems with competing objectives.

04 / Practice

Teaching & technical

Teaching has been part of my work since 2017. As an Assistant Lecturer at the University of Energy and Natural Resources, I taught undergraduate courses in programming and computing while supervising final-year projects — the part of the job I find most rewarding, because it is where students stop following instructions and start making their own decisions.

My approach is grounded in the same conviction that drives my research: technology is only as good as the human judgment surrounding it. I want students to leave a course able to reason about why a method fits a problem, not just how to run it.
